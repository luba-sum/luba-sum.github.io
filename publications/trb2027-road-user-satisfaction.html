Conference Paper

Using Video Data Analytics to Measure Road User Satisfaction Supplementing Traditional Survey-Based Approach

Accepted for presentation

AuthorsZidan, M. R., Nurain, R., Joty, S. S., Tabassum, L., Raihan, M. A., Hossain, M.
Manuscript TRBAM-27-05993.
Venue106th Annual Meeting of the Transportation Research Board (TRB 2027), Washington, D.C.
StatusAccepted for presentation

Abstract

Objectives: This study aimed to develop a video-based Road User Satisfaction Index (RUSI) to supplement traditional survey-based satisfaction assessment for a mixed-traffic corridor, which enabled continuous, less subjective measurement where repeated surveys are difficult and road conditions keep changing.

Methods: Open-ended survey responses were analyzed using TF-IDF (Term Frequency-Inverse Document Frequency), literature integration, and manual review to identify five candidate constructs: Congestion and Mobility, Infrastructure Quality, Safety and Security, Traffic Management, and Comfort and User Experience, which were subsequently examined through deductive thematic analysis and retained. A Likert-scale questionnaire was developed from these constructs, with PLS-SEM (Partial Least Squares Structural Equation Modeling) used to assess the measurement model and PCA (Principal Component Analysis) derived weights applied to construct scores to calculate the composite RUSI. Furthermore, a video-based RUSI was developed using computer vision techniques through vehicle detection, tracking, and infrastructure segmentation, measuring the same constructs through indicators such as speed, headway, braking, time-to-collision, footpath continuity, and median coverage.

Findings: The survey model showed strong reliability with Traffic Management the strongest driver of both Safety and Comfort. The survey-based RUSI was 39.1 out of 100, while the video-based RUSI was 55.4; this 16.3-point gap was mainly explained by subjective factors, such as perceived security and emotional experience.

Novelty: This study used video data analytics as a continuous supplement to traditional survey-based satisfaction assessment for a mixed-traffic corridor, offering an empirical basis for understanding how far objective, continuously observable conditions diverge from subjective, periodically surveyed user experience.

Practical Applications: Automated RUSI framework skeleton has been developed. Spatial and temporal calibration will enrich the framework in time, and will be industry-ready for pragmatic use.

Keywords: Road user satisfaction; Video analytics; Computer vision; PLS-SEM; TF-IDF; Mixed traffic; Key performance indicators

Study Design

The study corridor is the Dhaka–Mymensingh Highway in the Uttara–BNS section, chosen because it represents the heterogeneous, non-lane-based traffic typical of Dhaka. Satisfaction was measured two ways on the same corridor: a survey route (open-ended responses analysed with TF-IDF and deductive thematic analysis, a Likert questionnaire assessed with PLS-SEM, and a PCA-weighted composite index scaled 0–100) and a video route (vehicle detection and tracking with RF-DETR and ByteTrack, and infrastructure segmentation with YOLOv8s-seg to measure the same five constructs from observable indicators).

For vehicle detection, RF-DETR (Large) reached an mAP50 of 84.8% against 69.7% for YOLOv8n trained on the same data, and was selected for the video pipeline.
