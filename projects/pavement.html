Research project

Automated Pavement Anomaly Detection and Asset Management through Video Analytics

October 2025 – present · With Imperial College London and LGED Bangladesh

RoleGraduate Researcher
LabMoinul Hossain, Ph.D. Lab, Islamic University of Technology
PartnersDr. Mohammed Quddus, Imperial College London; Local Government Engineering Department (LGED), Bangladesh
MethodsYOLOv11 instance segmentation, GPS telemetry, ROADROID IRI, chainage-based data fusion, QGIS, GeoJSON/Shapefile
CoverageAbout 2,700 km across two districts

Overview

Pavement condition on the rural and local road network is still largely assessed through manual visual inspection, which is slow, subjective and difficult to repeat. This project is building an automated workflow for the Local Government Engineering Department (LGED), Bangladesh, in collaboration with Dr. Mohammed Quddus at Imperial College London. It collects synchronised video, motion-sensor, GPS and roughness data from survey vehicles and converts them into a geo-referenced inventory of pavement defects for asset management.

Objectives

  • Collect synchronised road-condition, motion-sensor, GPS and video data from survey vehicles.
  • Build a unified dataset for pavement analysis and defect detection.
  • Produce an automated, map-based defect inventory that supports the move from manual inspection to digital asset management.

Data Collection System

All devices are mounted on the survey vehicle and record at the same frequency so that every frame of video can be tied to a location, a roughness value and the vehicle’s motion state.

Field Survey Operations

I configured and tested the equipment on the survey vehicles, wrote the surveyor deployment protocol, and ran a three-day training programme for surveyors to standardise data collection across two districts covering about 2,700 km of road. I also trained the team on image annotation and dataset preparation in Roboflow for the detection models.

Defect Detection Pipeline

  • Model. A YOLOv11 instance-segmentation model (large), fine-tuned on annotated survey frames, segments potholes, cracking and edge-breaks in the GoPro video.
  • Data fusion. Each detection is matched to the GPS telemetry and to the roughness record, and results are aggregated at a 20 m chainage resolution along each road.
  • Inventory. Every defect is stored with its type, severity, area, model confidence, location and an identifier, giving a production road-defect inventory rather than a set of annotated videos.

GIS and Reporting

A companion GIS pipeline checks network coverage against the planned survey routes and exports the results automatically as GeoJSON, Shapefile and interactive HTML maps. The maps show an IRI heat-map along the road, with poor-condition sections in red, and individual defect records that can be opened for their attributes.

Status

Ongoing. Field data collection and the detection and GIS pipelines are in use. Model refinement and network-level reporting are continuing.
